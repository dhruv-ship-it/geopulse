The check says FAIL because it counts rows in `incidents` and expects 1. It found 2. What those
two rows are matters more than the count:

  INC-7833bfd098e220b5   opened 2485 s, closed 10865 s   peak 62 members
  INC-2702da1e86941e50   opened 10840 s, closed 10855 s  peak 3 members

The first one IS the fault. It opens as the region degrades, holds every one of the 62 labelled
zones, and lives for 8,380 seconds - the injected fault ran from 2160 s to 10800 s. That is the
project's central claim working: 62 co-degrading zones reported as one thing.

The second is a 15-second fragment at the decay. As the fault heals unevenly the surviving
degraded zones briefly stop being one connected component, and one of the islands reached
INCIDENT_MIN_ZONES (3, exactly) long enough to be named before it closed.

It is left in rather than tuned out. Raising INCIDENT_CLOSE_GRACE_MS, raising INCIDENT_MIN_ZONES,
or suppressing splits from a dissolving parent would each make this file print PASS, and choosing
one of them because it makes an assertion pass is how a measurement stops meaning anything. The
honest position is that the correlation is right and the split policy is one notch too eager at
the tail of an incident.
