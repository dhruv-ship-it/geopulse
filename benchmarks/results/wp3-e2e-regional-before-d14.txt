WHAT THIS FILE IS
================================================================

The run that found defect D14. Kept because the failure is more instructive than the fix.

Read the two clusters in the incident table. The fault was injected from 2160 s to 10800 s of
event time — 2.4 simulated hours — but every incident here lives in one of two narrow bands:

  2485 s .. 2680 s    (the onset)
  10750 s .. 10865 s  (the decay)

Between them, 8,070 seconds in which the system had no incident open at all, while 62 zones sat
continuously STRESSED or CRITICAL.

The cause is a mismatch between two components that were each behaving correctly:

  - stream-processor is EDGE-triggered. StateMachine.shouldAlert fires only when a zone CHANGES
    state. 62 zones crossed into STRESSED over ~80 seconds, and then said nothing for the next
    2.2 hours, because nothing changed.

  - the correlation window is LEVEL-expecting. Its membership rule is "a zone is an active member
    while it has degraded within the last CORRELATION_WINDOW_MS" (120 s).

So the engine's event-time watermark froze at 2555 s, its window expired every member at 2680 s,
and the incident closed in the middle of an ongoing fault. The stats show the watermark frozen
and then fast-forwarding 1,610 ticks when the recovery messages finally arrived.

The seven incidents are then: four as the onset component formed and expired unevenly, and three
more at the decay, where CRITICAL -> STRESSED is still a degradation and re-admitted zones that
had long since been forgotten.

The fix is DEGRADATION_REASSERT_MS: a still-degraded zone re-publishes its degradation every
30 s of event time, which is what the window was always assuming it would. See
stream-processor/src/streamProcessor.ts and ADR-008.

The passing run is benchmarks/results/wp3-e2e-regional.txt.
